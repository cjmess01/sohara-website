Competitive Advantage & VRIO

The SoHara Sentinel is Valuable, Rare, and Inimitable.

Value: Through the value propositions of safety, extended battery lifespan, and increased reusability of batteries in BESS, the Sentinel provides unparalled value to BESS manufacturers.

Rarity: The offering of real time and high-quality SoH estimation is not currently available for BESS. SoHara Sentinel is providing a unique and rare capability.

Imitability: SoHara, through an exclusive patent licensing agreement with The University of Alabama, has guaranteed protection of proprietary methods.

SoHara is presently positioned to capitalize upon the potential of the Sentinel.

Organizational Alignment: SoHara has the proper structure, management systems, incentives, processes, culture, and leadership alignment to create and commercialize the Sentinel.

5 Forces

Competitive rivalry is high among BMS suppliers, but most incumbents focus on basic protection and energy estimation rather than physics based diagnostics.

Supplier power is low because SoHara relies on commodity electronics and compute platforms rather than scarce materials.

Buyer power is high since large OEMs and integrators control high volume programs and are sensitive to cost and integration risk.

Threat of new entrants ismoderate in software only analytics, but combining embedded EIS with validated models and field data raises the barrier to entry.

The threat of substitutes is mainly competing monitoring approaches, such as purely data driven cloud analytics or oversizing batteries to manage uncertainty, both of which are expensive or less precise than directly measuring electrochemical response.

PESTEL Highlights

Political: From the right side of the political spectrum, there is a growing call for America to become energy independent. Likewise, the left side is pushing America towards renewable energy applications, as seen in U.S. incentives for clean energy credits from the IRA. The Sentinel benefits from both these trends, as it will allow less reliance on imports critical to battery production, like cobalt and tin, which come from China. From the left, renewable energy sources like wind and solar energy rely on BESS, which are made safer and more reliable by the Sentinel.

Economic: The BESS market is projected to grow from $44 billion in 2023 to $120 billion in 2030. The Sentinel is well positioned to take advantage of this trend.

Social: A key bottleneck to BESS adoption is ongoing safety concerns, which have caused destructive fires and disasters in California, South Korea, and elsewhere. The Sentinel is positioned to take advantage of these concerns through its safety-providing features.

Technological: The rapid advancement of lithium-ion and solid state technology have created a market for better SoH solutions. Also, the increased energy demands from AI application and cryptocurrency mining create demand for BMS.

Environmental: A continued focus on renewables creates more demand for battery systems, which require BMS products like the Sentinel.

Legal: Strict compliance standards and utility regulations present a threat to Sentinel adoption.

Scenario Planning

Optimistic Scenario: SoHara is awarded the STTR grant and partners with The University of Alabama to construct a FGP version of the Sentinel. SoHara enters a pilot program with a BESS manufacturer, for example LeClanche or Smartville. Adoption thtroughout the southeast commences, adding value to the regional battery infrastructure. Within 3 years, SoHara has a meaningful footprint in the southeast, with aims to expand nationwide and beyond.

Medium Scenario: SoHara is awarded the STTR grant and partners with The University of Alabama to construct a FGP version of the Sentinel. SoHara is unable to enter into a partnership agreement with a BESS manufacturer, slowly the rate of adoption of the Sentinel. Within 3 years, SoHara has made some sales to a state BESS entity like Alabama Power.

Poor Scenario: SoHara is not awarded the STTR grant. Instead, SoHara will seek funding through private equity connections made through an accelerator, for example the Bronze Valley Accelerator in Birmingham or the Techstars Accelerator, also in Birmingham. Development is significantly more expensive due to lack of access to UA resources and expertise.

Financial Projections

SoHara projects profitability to occur in year 3 of operation. Prior to this, funding through government grants like the NSF STTR or Accelerators like Birmingham's Bronze Valley Accelerator will be pursued. A lean balance sheet consisting of mainly equipment and buildings assets (with minimal inventory) will ensure SoHara has the flexibility to move with market demands.
